THE PORTFOLIO

Eight pathways to climate resilience

Choose the intervention only after confirming the site, baseline, affected population, delivery partner, owner and permissions. Outcomes below are measures to verify, not forecasts or promises.

01 / WATER SECURITY

Safe, reliable drinking water

Assess dry-season gaps, water quality, losses and source sustainability. Compare network repair and demand management before new extraction or recharge.

Beneficiaries
Unique households with improved verified supply
Measured change
Service days, water-test compliance, time spent collecting water
Delivery window
3–6 months assessment/design; 6–18 months potential implementation
02 / WATERSHEDS

Catchment and soil restoration

Target surveyed drainage units, soil cover and groundwater pathways. Check ecological suitability, permissions and convergence with public schemes.

Beneficiaries
Farm households and hectares demonstrably treated
Measured change
Soil loss, infiltration, seasonal water availability and income
Delivery window
3–5 years programme horizon where appropriate
03 / HEAT

Cool and safe public buildings

Measure actual exposure at schools, clinics and workplaces; evaluate shading, roof systems, ventilation and occupational heat plans.

Beneficiaries
Documented building occupants and workers
Measured change
Occupied hours above threshold, indoor heat, electricity
Delivery window
2–4 months monitoring/design; 3–9 months works and checks
04 / FLOOD SAFETY

Drainage and early action

Identify encroached flow paths, flood exposures, shelters and warning last-mile gaps; avoid transferring flooding to neighbouring areas.

Beneficiaries
Households/critical services with reduced verified exposure
Measured change
Flood depth/duration, warning lead time, access uptime
Delivery window
6–24 months including permissions and seasonal testing
05 / LAKES & WETLANDS

Recover waterbody function

Survey legal boundaries, biodiversity, catchment inflows, water quality and sewage loads before earthworks. Restore function rather than count desilting alone.

Beneficiaries
Verified water users and ecologically restored waterbodies
Measured change
Area, wet-season storage, water quality, species and upkeep
Delivery window
6–24 months works; multi-season ecological follow-up
06 / FARMS & LIVESTOCK

Climate-resilient livelihoods

Test soil, water, heat and fodder constraints with a representative farmer group. Compare low-regret management changes against capital-intensive options.

Beneficiaries
Unique farmers and livestock owners; animals counted separately
Measured change
Net income, crop loss, water per hectare, animal health
Delivery window
2–3 crop seasons to establish credible results
07 / COASTS & BIODIVERSITY

Nature-positive coastal protection

Start with local ecology, shoreline change and tenure; prioritize suitable habitat protection and managed restoration, not indiscriminate planting.

Beneficiaries
Coastal households and protected/restored habitat hectares
Measured change
Shoreline/habitat condition, survival, fishery-linked outcomes
Delivery window
2–5 years including repeat ecological surveys
08 / CLEAN ENERGY

Resilient essential services

Assess energy demand, grid reliability, efficient equipment and maintenance skills before specifying solar, storage or electric alternatives.

Beneficiaries
Unique people with reliable essential service
Measured change
Service uptime, metered energy and defensible tCO₂e
Delivery window
3–12 months delivery; annual emissions reconciliation

Planning durations above are POIGAI scoping assumptions, not commitments. They vary with project size, procurement, permits, monsoons and counterpart readiness.

IMPACT ASSURANCE

Count what changes.
Not just what is spent.

People

Unique direct recipients, household IDs, service quality, time saved, inclusion and grievances.

Livestock

Animals by species, animal-days with shade/water, recorded health outcomes.

Trees & nature

Existing trees protected, native trees planted and independently verified survival at 12/36 months.

Lakes & wetlands

Unique waterbody IDs, area, function, quality, biodiversity and post-monsoon upkeep.

Climate

Measured exposure, resilience services and tCO₂e only when the measurement boundary and factors are documented.

Finance

Committed versus disbursed funds, expenditure evidence, lifecycle cost and community maintenance provision.

Before and after: a defensible comparison

BEFORE / BASELINE

Record the actual households, water supply days, seasonal lake storage, building heat or farm yields—plus location, date, method and source.

AFTER / VERIFIED

Re-measure with the same indicators, seasonal conditions and quality controls. State observed change, uncertainty and what cannot be attributed to POIGAI.

There are no completed POIGAI interventions in this portfolio from which to claim real before–after improvements. Projections must be labelled scenarios until verified.

FROM SCREENING TO STEWARDSHIP

A gated project timeline

  1. 0–90 days · Validate

    Confirm district evidence, select a site with consent, identify demand and affected people, screen safeguards and permissions.

  2. Months 3–6 · Design

    Collect seasonal baseline as feasible; evaluate alternatives; identify implementation and maintenance owners; obtain priced BOQ and risk register.

  3. Months 6–9 · Financial readiness

    Independent design review, legal recipient due diligence, procurement plan, outcome agreement and milestone-based funding decision.

  4. Months 9–24 · Delivery where appropriate

    Procure and implement against approved milestones. Publish progress, variation controls, grievance channels and field verification.

  5. Years 2–5 · Maintain and evaluate

    Check performance through relevant seasons, evaluate equity and ecology, publish expenditure and aftercare accountability.

Not all projects fit this sequence; watershed and ecological restoration may need longer, while simple heat adaptation can be shorter. These are provisional planning windows, not announced project deadlines.

INTERNATIONAL FUNDER READINESS

Seven gates before an investment case.

01 Location. Precisely identified site, permissions, catchment and coordinates.

02 Baseline. Independent evidence of exposure, service and ecological condition.

03 Unmet need. Demonstrated gap after mapping public schemes and existing services.

04 Delivery partner. Capable legal implementer with references and safeguarding controls.

05 Maintenance owner. Signed accountability, staffing and recurrent finance.

06 Costed design. Itemised quantities, unit rates, contingencies, procurement and lifecycle cost.

07 Verifiable results. Baseline, targets, denominators, verification plan, risks and independent reporting.

POIGAI's role is to organize and qualify opportunities. Funding routes, recipient eligibility, CSR rules, applicable foreign-contribution law and donor tax implications require project-specific due diligence before any transfer. No funding is solicited or processed on this page.

PUBLIC EVIDENCE · METHODS

Trace the assumptions

National evidence informs initial screening; it does not replace district-specific hazard layers, local demand surveys or official plans.

  • CEEW · Mapping India's Climate Vulnerability (2021) — district vulnerability framework.
  • CEEW · District Heat Risk (2025) — 734 districts, 35 indicators.
  • DST · Flood & drought district risk report — hazard/exposure/vulnerability assessment.
  • NRSC/Bhuvan · Historical flood layers — interpretation subject to satellite coverage limits.
  • WDC–PMKSY 2.0 official guidelines — unit-cost norms and implementation structure.
